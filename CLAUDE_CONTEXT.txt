1. THE THESIS (1 paragraph)
----------------------------
Within the S&P 500, stocks whose ownership has been disproportionately captured
by passive / mandate-driven holders experience systematically slower
incorporation of fundamental information into prices, producing a tradable
post-news drift over weeks to months. The marginal price-setter has thinned, so
information diffuses at the speed of the remaining active pool. The full
README.md in this repo has the academic citations (Bernard & Thomas, Hong &
Stein, Hirshleifer & Teoh, Ben-David et al., Coles et al., Appel/Gormley/Keim).
Treat the thesis as a starting point — if the proxy data ends up suggesting a
cleaner variant, propose it.


2. HARD USER CONSTRAINTS (do not violate)
------------------------------------------
- No tactical asset allocation. No market timing, no regime overlays that
  shift exposure to/from cash based on macro signals.
- No derivatives. Cash-equity book only. No options, futures, swaps.
- Max 20% cash or fixed income at all times. The portfolio must be at least
  80% in equities, every day. If the strategy can't find candidate stocks,
  the residual goes into the S&P 500 TR (the benchmark), NOT cash.
- Long-only. No shorting.
- Universe: historical S&P 500 (point-in-time, survivorship-bias-free) over
  2005-2024. The 10yr (2015-2024) and 20yr (2005-2024) windows are the
  primary backtest horizons.


3. BLOOMBERG ENVIRONMENT REALITIES (discovered the hard way in R)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The terminal this code runs on has DAPI desktop access via BLPAPI. The
following gaps are real and PRECIPITATED most of our R debugging cycles:

ENTITLEMENT-LOCKED FIELDS (preflight against AAPL drops them silently):
- PASSIVELY_HELD_PCT_OUT       <-- the KEY field for the strategy
- ACTIVELY_HELD_PCT_OUT
- INSTITUTIONAL_PERCENT_HELD
- ETF_OWNED_PCT, INDEX_OWNED_PCT (also tried, also not entitled)

INVALID FIELD NAMES (BBG rejects them outright with "Bad field"):
- GROSS_PROFIT_MARGIN          <-- not a real BBG field; compute locally
                                   as GROSS_PROFIT / BS_TOT_ASSET
- BETA_ADJ_OVERRIDABLE         <-- requires an override; v1 doesn't use
                                   it anyway (CAPM regression is local)

FIELDS THAT WORK AND ARE USED:
- Universe:     INDX_MWEIGHT_HIST (bds), with END_DATE_OVERRIDE per quarter
- Reference:    GICS_SECTOR_NAME, GICS_INDUSTRY_NAME, ID_BB_GLOBAL (bdp)
- Prices:       PX_LAST, PX_VOLUME, EQY_SH_OUT, CUR_MKT_CAP (bdh, daily)
- Fundamentals: RETURN_COM_EQY, GROSS_PROFIT, BS_TOT_ASSET,
                TOT_DEBT_TO_TOT_EQY, EARN_GROWTH_TTM_STDEV (maybe),
                CF_CASH_FROM_OPER, ASSET_TURNOVER, PE_RATIO,
                PX_TO_BOOK_RATIO, EV_TO_T12M_EBITDA, CF_FREE_CASH_FLOW,
                FCF_YIELD, EARN_YLD (bdh, quarterly)
- News:         BEST_EPS, IS_EPS, BEST_SALES, SALES_REV_TURN,
                OPER_MARGIN, BEST_EPS_REVISION_RATIO_1M (bdh, quarterly)
- Ownership:    EQY_FLOAT  (the only ownership field that works)
- Macro:        SPXT Index, SPXEW Index, USGG3M Index (bdh, daily, PX_LAST)

UNIVERSE FORMAT GOTCHA:
INDX_MWEIGHT_HIST returns tickers in mixed formats. Many are Bloomberg
"delisted GUIDs" with digit prefixes like "0111145D US Equity" — these
are historical S&P 500 members whose original ticker no longer exists.
They WILL return historical data for the years they were active. The
canonical normaliser strips any trailing " Equity" and any 1-3 letter
exchange code and re-appends " US Equity". After normalisation, the
20-year universe has ~940 unique tickers, of which roughly half are
delisted GUIDs.

BDH CHUNKING:
Bloomberg's per-request data-point cap is not documented but is in the
neighbourhood of a few hundred thousand points. A naive pull of 50
tickers × 7 fields × 5000 days returns silently empty. Use chunks of
10 tickers × 5-year date windows.


4. THE PASSIVE-OWNERSHIP PROXY (key methodological choice)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Because PASSIVELY_HELD_PCT_OUT is entitlement-locked, the R prototype's
final passive-intensity score is a defensible proxy:

    passive_intensity_proxy
        = z_in_sector( log(90d avg market_cap) )           [size leg]
        + z_in_sector( -1 * 90d avg PX_VOLUME/EQY_FLOAT )  [turnover leg]

both terms z-scored within GICS sector, averaged equal-weight.

Defensibility:
- Size within sector: Appel, Gormley & Keim (2016, JFE) "Passive
  Investors, Not Passive Owners" uses end-of-May market cap as the
  Russell 1000/2000 assignment variable — same identification chain.
- Float-adjusted turnover: low PX_VOLUME / EQY_FLOAT is a direct
  microstructure signal of a buy-and-hold holder base. Float-adjusting
  (vs raw shares outstanding) is important — raw turnover misclassifies
  controlled-ownership stocks (founder stakes, Berkshire-type
  structures) as "passive-heavy" when they are just illiquid.

The new Python code should keep a dual-path design: if
PASSIVELY_HELD_PCT_OUT EVER becomes available, use it directly; else
fall back to the proxy. Switch at runtime based on what the BDH
preflight returns.


5. WHAT THE R PROTOTYPE DELIVERED, AND WHAT IT GOT WRONG
---------------------------------------------------------
Repository layout on the latest branch:

    config/        settings.R, bbg_fields.R
    bloomberg/     6 extraction scripts (universe, prices, fundamentals,
                   news, ownership, macro) + orchestrator
    R/             data_loader, signals/{fundamental_news, passive_intensity,
                   quality, value}, portfolio, backtest, metrics,
                   visualization
    data/raw/      cached parquet outputs of the BBG extractions
    data/universe/ cached SPX historical constituent panel
    output/        figures, tables

What v1 delivered:
- Survivorship-bias-free historical universe (~940 tickers)
- Daily price panel (~3M rows, ~50 MB parquet)
- Quarterly fundamentals + news signals
- Quality and Value risk-control screens
- Composite news x passive signal
- Walk-forward backtest with explicit lookahead guard
- Transaction-cost drag, sector neutrality with tolerance, position cap,
  turnover buffer
- Performance metrics: CAGR, vol, Sharpe, Sortino, max DD, Calmar, CVaR,
  Ulcer, hit rate, CAPM alpha/beta, IR

What v1 got wrong (and v2 partially fixes):
- The composite was news_z * passive_z. Negative * negative = positive,
  so genuinely bad-news stocks in low-passive sectors scored well.
  v2 default is rank_product (both in [0,1], no sign-flip).
- The quality/value/news filters dropped tickers whenever the score was
  NA, not just when explicitly bad. With BBG entitlement gaps producing
  many NA scores, this collapsed the candidate universe to zero on
  every rebalance. The strategy ended up holding 100% cash for the
  entire backtest (CAGR ~1.8%, matching the 3M T-bill rate, beta to
  market ~0).
- v2 relaxes the filters: NA-scored stocks pass through; news
  eligibility is "X-of-non-NA" instead of "X-of-all"; engine enforces a
  hard 80% equity floor by filling the residual with SPX TR if
  selection returns empty.

WE DID NOT GET TO RUN v2 IN R BEFORE THE PYTHON RESTART, so we do not
know if the v2 fixes actually deliver a non-cash portfolio. That's the
first thing the Python version should verify.

7. PYTHON API RECOMMENDATION
----------------------------
PRIMARY:
    blpapi (Python bindings of Bloomberg's BLPAPI). The direct analog of
    Rblpapi. Documentation:
        https://bloomberg.github.io/blpapi-docs/python/3.26.3/
    Canonical request flow:
        import blpapi
        opts = blpapi.SessionOptions()
        opts.setServerHost("localhost"); opts.setServerPort(8194)
        sess = blpapi.Session(opts); sess.start()
        sess.openService("//blp/refdata")
        svc = sess.getService("//blp/refdata")
        req = svc.createRequest("HistoricalDataRequest")
        req.getElement("securities").appendValue("AAPL US Equity")
        req.getElement("fields").appendValue("PX_LAST")
        req.set("startDate", "20200101"); req.set("endDate", "20241231")
        req.set("periodicitySelection", "DAILY")
        sess.sendRequest(req)
        # then loop on sess.nextEvent(), check eventType in
        # {PARTIAL_RESPONSE, RESPONSE}, extract message elements.

    Three request types cover everything we need:
        - ReferenceDataRequest    (current values + bulk fields like
                                   INDX_MWEIGHT_HIST)
        - HistoricalDataRequest   (daily / quarterly time series)
        - IntradayBarRequest      (not needed for this strategy)

ALTERNATIVE (preferred if entitled):
    BQuant / "A Python API for Investment Research". Higher-level
    Pythonic interface designed for exactly this kind of factor /
    backtest workflow. If your team has BQuant entitlement, prefer it
    over raw blpapi.

THIRD-PARTY WRAPPERS (consider but vet):
    - xbbg, pdblp — pandas-friendly wrappers over blpapi. Faster to
      iterate with but you give up some control. Fine for the
      extraction layer; not necessary for the analytics layer.

DO NOT INSTALL OR USE:
    - Any derivatives-pricing API (no derivatives constraint)
    - Any OMS / order-routing API (this is research, not execution)
    - Any subscription / real-time tick API (the strategy is daily-bar)


8. WHAT YOU CAN PROPOSE TO CHANGE (thesis-open items)
------------------------------------------------------
The user is explicitly open to thesis and strategy revisions. Items that
are reasonable to question:

- Composite formula. rank_product is a v2 default — rank_sum,
  rank_intersection, weighted-by-signal-IC, or a 3-component score that
  adds momentum or value alongside news+passive are all on the table.
- Universe. Historical SPX is methodologically clean. Russell 1000
  expands the universe by ~2x. Excluding the bottom 100 names by
  market cap is also reasonable.
- Rebalance cadence. Quarterly is the v1 default. Monthly would track
  the news signal faster but increase turnover. Worth testing.
- Position sizing. Inverse-vol within sector is v1. Risk parity,
  minimum variance, or equal-weight within the long book are all
  defensible. Volatility-targeted gross exposure (cap at 1.0) is
  another option that keeps the 80% floor while controlling realised
  vol.
- The thesis itself. If the proxy passive-intensity (size + low
  turnover) makes the strategy effectively a low-vol-plus-quality
  bet rather than a distinct passive-ownership-drift bet, the honest
  framing of the strategy should be revised in the README. The R
  prototype's notes/V1_BACKTEST_GUIDE flagged this as a known risk to
  honestly disclose.

WHAT IS NOT NEGOTIABLE:
    The three constraints in section 2. Everything else is up for
    discussion.

11. KNOWN R-SIDE BUGS YOU DON'T NEED TO REINVENT
-------------------------------------------------
Just avoid these by design:
- BDS returns mixed-case columns (BDP returns uppercase, BDS lowercased
  ours). Lowercase every column at extraction time.
- Parquet writes fail on Windows if the file is mmap'd by a prior read
  in the same process. Read with mmap=False, or close the file handle
  before re-writing.
- max() on empty vectors warns and returns -Inf; guard with
  `if len(x) == 0: ...`.
- Bloomberg "date" column comes back as the first column with default
  case "date" but can vary across versions; always detect by name
  match, never by index position.
- Trailing whitespace and double-suffixed tickers ("AAPL US Equity US
  Equity") silently break BDH; canonicalise tickers ONCE at extraction
  time and only ever pass canonical form to BBG.
